New-store readiness

Cutting new-store training from 14 days to 5

The original model required 14 days of on-site delivery for every opening. The redesign moved foundational knowledge to pre-work, kept hands-on practice live, and developed local training capacity.

RoleProgram redesign, eLearning integration, training-store certification, regional trainer development, and rollout with Operations and field leadership
ProblemEvery opening required two full weeks of on-site trainer delivery
DeliverableA blended model with asynchronous pre-work, five live training days, eight certified training stores, and 12 regional trainers and training leaders
Result64% reduction in training time, 39 openings supported, and an estimated $56K in trainer lodging avoided

Why 14 days became a constraint

Every new Sandbox VR location used a 14-day, fully on-site training program. Openings increased from 3 in 2023 to 19 in 2025, with 10 more in the first quarter of 2026.

Each opening required two weeks of trainer travel and delivery. As more openings overlapped across regions, trainer availability and travel became part of the scheduling decision.

Foundational knowledge moved earlier, hands-on practice stayed live, and more delivery moved closer to each market

Responsibilities across the redesign

I led the redesign from the Field Trainer role through my current Senior Global L&D Program Manager role. The work included deciding what moved to eLearning, what stayed live, how training stores were certified, how regional trainers were prepared, and how readiness would be checked.

Operations, Deployment, store managers, regional trainers, Directors of Stores, and the Senior Vice President of Stores were involved in the rollout and the decisions about local delivery.

What had to stay in person

Live time remained focused on coached guest-journey practice, technical recovery, floor scenarios, feedback, and observed certification. These activities required equipment, repetition, and a trainer who could watch the performance.

What moved earlier

Foundational product, process, and safety knowledge moved into the eLearning catalog before arrival. That reduced the lecture time required during the live schedule and left the five on-site days for practice.

What moved closer to the field

Eight stores were certified as training locations, and 12 regional trainers and training leaders were prepared to deliver the shared program closer to their markets.

The five-day model

The live schedule used the same learning plan, practice sequence, observation criteria, and certification requirements across markets. Trainers could adjust coaching and repetition while retaining the same performance standard.

How quality was checked

Readiness remained tied to observed performance, scenario practice, and certification evidence. The New Store Opening Portal later added a shared place to record those measures across the first five portal-supported openings.

What changed

  • Training time dropped 64%, from 14 days to 5.
  • The blended model was used across 39 location openings between 2023 and Q1 2026: 3 in 2023, 7 in 2024, 19 in 2025, and 10 in Q1 2026.
  • Eight certified training stores and 12 regional trainers and training leaders could deliver the shared program closer to each market.
  • An estimated $56,000 in trainer lodging avoided, before counting airfare, per diem, ground travel, or trainer time. This is a directional estimate, not a booked budget reduction.
  • Foundational content, the live learning plan, observation criteria, and trainer certification were shared across markets.

Estimate basis: roughly nine fewer hotel nights per opening across 39 openings, at approximately the 2025 U.S. average nightly rate of $160.
