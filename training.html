New-store readiness

Cutting new-store training from 14 days to 5

A blended, practice-heavy readiness model built around how adults learn—and what new teams actually need before opening day.

RoleProgram strategy, operating-model design, stakeholder alignment, and rollout
Challenge14-day on-site training was the bottleneck on every new-store opening
What I didRebuilt the model from all-live to blended, with async pre-work and local training capacity
Result64% reduction in training time, 39 openings supported, and an estimated $56K in trainer lodging avoided

The challenge

Every new Sandbox VR location opened on the back of a 14-day, fully on-site training program. It worked, but it did not scale.

As the company accelerated, the model became the constraint. Openings jumped from 3 in 2023 to 19 in 2025, with 10 more in the first quarter of 2026 alone. Each opening pulled trainers into two straight weeks of travel and delivery, delayed the date a store could start generating revenue, and left quality dependent on which trainer happened to be in the room.

The old model could not handle that volume without burning out the training team or letting consistency slip. Shortening the agenda would not be enough; the learning experience itself had to change.

The redesign: protect on-site time for hands-on practice, move everything else earlier or local

My role

I led the redesign from the first diagnosis through rollout, growing from Field Trainer into the Senior Global L&D Program Manager role during the work. I set the learning strategy, worked with operations and field leaders on the new model, built the supporting content and standards, and developed local trainers so every opening would not depend on one central team.

The hard part was deciding what people truly needed to learn live, setting a clear readiness bar, and giving local trainers ownership without creating a different experience in every market.

The approach

I used Knowles’ Andragogy to make the experience relevant, self-directed, and centered on real job problems. Learners needed to understand why each standard mattered, connect new procedures to what they already knew, and spend live time practicing rather than listening. Everything that did not require a trainer or the physical environment moved earlier or moved local.

Shifted foundational knowledge to async pre-work

I built a 54-module eLearning catalog from the ground up, from instructional design and scripting through filming, voiceover, editing, and LMS deployment. Learners could complete the foundations at their own pace and arrive knowing both the core procedures and why they mattered. That left the five on-site days for coached practice and floor readiness instead of lecture.

Built local training capacity

I established eight certified training stores and developed 12 regional trainers and training leaders. Their development followed an Action Learning rhythm: work through real opening challenges, take action in the field, compare what happened, and use that reflection to improve the next launch. Certification standards and coaching kept the readiness bar consistent while local leaders built their own judgment.

Standardized the on-site portion

With the foundations handled before arrival, the five on-site days used scenarios, coached repetitions, observation, and reflection. The experience was consistent across markets without turning trainers into people who simply read from a script.

The outcome

  • Training time dropped 64%, from 14 days to 5.
  • The model supported 39 location openings between 2023 and Q1 2026 (3 in 2023, 7 in 2024, 19 in 2025, and 10 in Q1 2026), a volume the original approach could not have absorbed.
  • More training happened locally. Eight certified training stores and 12 regional trainers and training leaders created capacity closer to each market.
  • An estimated $56,000 in trainer lodging avoided, before counting airfare, per diem, ground travel, or trainer time. This is an estimate based on avoided hotel nights, not a booked budget reduction.
  • Openings were no longer bottlenecked by trainer travel, freeing the team to support more launches in parallel.
  • Delivery became more consistent across markets, because the foundation was standardized and local trainers were certified to the same bar.

Estimate basis: roughly nine fewer hotel nights per opening across 39 openings, at approximately the 2025 U.S. average nightly rate of $160.
