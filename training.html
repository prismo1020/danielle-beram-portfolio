New-store readiness

Cutting new-store training from 14 days to 5

I cut new-store training from two weeks to five days. Stores opened sooner, and trainers spent less time on the road.

RoleProgram design and rollout
Challenge14-day on-site training was the bottleneck on every new-store opening
What I didMoved early learning online and built a network of local trainers
ResultTraining took 64% less time. The model supported 39 openings and avoided an estimated $56K in hotel costs.

The challenge

Each new Sandbox VR location started with 14 days of live, on-site training. That schedule worked when openings were rare. As the company grew, the trainer calendar began to drive the opening schedule.

Openings rose from 3 in 2023 to 19 in 2025. Ten more followed in the first quarter of 2026. Trainers spent two full weeks away from home for each launch. Stores had to wait for an open spot on their travel calendars.

We needed a shorter program that kept the same readiness standard.

The five live days focus on practice. Learners finish the basics before they arrive.

My role

I owned the redesign and rollout. During this work, I moved from Field Trainer into the Senior Global L&D Program Manager role. I worked with operations leaders to set the new readiness standard and built the course content that supported it.

I had to decide which skills needed a live trainer and which lessons could move online. Then I taught local trainers how to use the same readiness check in every market.

The approach

I kept the five live days for work that needs a trainer or the store itself. That choice follows Knowles’ Andragogy. When you’re learning a new role, you want to know why a step matters and how it connects to what you already know. The program gives you that context before you arrive.

Shifted foundational knowledge to async pre-work

I built 54 modules and loaded them into the LMS. Trainees completed the basics before they traveled. When they arrived, trainers could start with coached practice on the floor.

Built local training capacity

I certified eight training stores and developed 12 regional trainers and training leaders. They learned through live opening problems. They tried a solution, then met as a group to talk through what worked. That’s Revans’ Action Learning in plain terms.

Standardized the on-site portion

Every market used the same five-day plan and readiness check. Trainers still had room to coach the person in front of them.

The outcome

  • Training time fell 64%, from 14 days to 5.
  • The model supported 39 openings between 2023 and the first quarter of 2026.
  • Eight certified training stores brought support closer to each market.
  • The shorter trips avoided an estimated $56,000 in hotel costs. That estimate doesn’t include airfare or trainer time.
  • Opening dates no longer depended on one central trainer’s travel calendar.
  • Each market used the same course foundation and certification standard.

Estimate: nine fewer hotel nights for each of 39 openings at the 2025 U.S. average nightly rate of about $160.
