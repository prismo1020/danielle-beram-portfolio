Apollo: an AI support chatbot for 90 locations

When you’re running a live shift, you can’t wait on a support ticket. Apollo gives staff an answer from approved company content.

RoleProduct direction and content ownership
ChallengeFrontline staff had no fast, consistent way to get operational answers mid-shift
What I didBuilt Apollo and the source library behind it
ResultTeams at 90 locations can get help in 5 languages. Process-confusion escalations fell as training and support improved.

The challenge

When you hit a question during a live shift, you need an answer right away. Staff had to open a support ticket or interrupt a manager. They could search through SOPs, but those files lived across several tools.

Across 90 locations, staff got different answers depending on who they asked. Process confusion caused about 40% of all escalated tickets.

My role

I chose what Apollo should answer and how it should use company content. I built the first version and saw how the way staff ask a question can change the answer they get.

I work with subject owners across the company. Each source has a clear owner, and new information reaches Apollo as the work changes.

The approach

A good answer starts with clear source content. The chat window is only the last step.

Apollo points staff to approved content, so each location gets the same answer.

The approach, continued

Break long SOPs into focused answers

I split long documents into short entries that answer one question at a time. Each entry has a clear title and owner. That makes it easier for Apollo to find the right instruction.

Use approved company content

Apollo uses source material the company has reviewed. Staff can trace an answer back to that source when they need more detail.

Keep each answer current

Each of the 300-plus resources has an owner. I review changes and update Apollo when a process changes.

Make it available to every store

Apollo serves all 90 locations in five languages.

The outcome

The drop reflects the full training and support system. Apollo is one part of it.

  • Staff at 90 locations can get help in five languages during a shift.
  • Process-confusion escalations fell from about 40% to about 5% as the full training and support system improved.
  • Each location gets the same approved answer.
  • Managers and support staff spend less time answering repeat questions.
