Apollo: an AI support chatbot for 90 locations

Giving frontline staff instant, accurate operational answers in the middle of a shift, in five languages.

RoleProduct strategy, knowledge architecture, governance, and network adoption
ChallengeFrontline staff had no fast, consistent way to get operational answers mid-shift
What I didBuilt and govern Apollo, an AI chatbot grounded in a retrieval-optimized knowledge base
ResultReal-time guidance across 90 locations and 5 languages; a contributor to a broader enablement-system decline in process-confusion escalations

The challenge

When a game guide or manager hit an operational question in the middle of a live shift, their options were all slow. Escalate a support ticket and wait. Interrupt a busy manager. Or dig through scattered SOPs across multiple platforms, in the middle of running the floor.

Across 90 corporate and franchise locations operating in five languages, that added up to a constant stream of avoidable escalations and inconsistent answers, where the response you got depended on who you happened to ask. Knowledge existed, but it was not reachable at the moment of need. Process confusion accounted for roughly 40% of all escalated tickets.

My role

I set the product direction for Apollo and lead the operating model behind it: use-case definition, knowledge architecture, answer boundaries, content ownership, governance, and adoption across the network. I also built the initial implementation, which gave me direct insight into how information structure and frontline behavior affect answer quality.

The work requires ongoing partnership with operations, product, support, and subject-matter owners. My role is to turn those inputs into one trusted experience while maintaining clear accountability for what Apollo can answer and how quickly its source content changes.

The approach

The goal was not just a chatbot. It was a reliable answer at the moment of need, which meant the retrieval had to be as good as the interface.

Answers come from governed source content, not open-ended generation, so they stay accurate and consistent

The approach, continued

Architected the knowledge base for retrieval, not just storage

I structured the underlying content into governed, retrieval-optimized snippets so Apollo returns precise, relevant answers instead of vague or partial ones. Good AI answers start with well-organized source content, so I treated the knowledge base as the real product.

Built the assistant on Chatbase, grounded in that knowledge base

Apollo answers from governed source material rather than open-ended generation, which keeps responses accurate, consistent, and defensible. Staff get the same correct answer regardless of which location or language they are working in.

Built a governance model so answers stay trustworthy

I define what Apollo can and cannot answer, coordinate ownership of the 300-plus resources behind it, and maintain the guardrails that prevent stale or off-scope responses. An AI assistant is only as good as the discipline behind its content, so governance is a permanent operating rhythm, not a launch task.

Deployed network-wide and multilingual

Apollo serves all 90 locations in five languages, putting the same quality of answer in front of every staff member on every shift.

The outcome

System-wide result across training, knowledge governance, and Apollo; not attributed to Apollo alone

  • Real-time operational guidance across 90 locations and 5 languages, available the instant a question comes up rather than after a ticket cycle.
  • Contributed to a system-wide decline in process-confusion escalations from roughly 40% to about 5%. This result reflects the combined effect of training redesign, knowledge governance, and Apollo; it is not attributed to the chatbot alone.
  • Consistent answers across the network. The response no longer depends on which manager is nearby or which language a location operates in.
  • Less load on managers and the support team, who were freed from repetitive frontline questions to focus on higher-value work.
