# Danielle Beram Brand Kit

## Editorial principle

**Let the work explain the work.**

The portfolio should sound like an experienced Learning & Development leader explaining work she completed. Use operational details, named artifacts, comparison groups, and measured results before interpretation. The visual identity should remain human, structured, and evidence-led.

## Public summary

### Primary statement

I build training programs people use.

### Supporting statement

My recent work includes global onboarding, new-store training, eLearning production, and an internal AI knowledge system.

### Short descriptor

Senior Global Learning & Development Program Manager

### Evidence standard

State the problem, role, artifact, result, comparison basis, and limitation.

## Personality

- Grounded, not corporate
- Direct, not performative
- Warm, not soft
- Ambitious, not inflated
- Technical, not jargon-heavy
- Evidence-led, not sterile
- Confident, not self-important

## Voice

### Use

- Short declarative sentences
- Concrete verbs: built, created, reduced, launched, measured, approved, corrected
- Specific scope and constraints
- Plain explanations of technical decisions
- Evidence immediately after a claim
- First-person ownership when the work was Danielle's

### Avoid

- Abstract noun stacks
- Generic leadership slogans
- "Passionate professional"
- "Leveraged synergies"
- "Drove transformation" without explaining what changed
- Repeating Danielle's name in every label
- Calling real work fictional when only the public data is sanitized

## Typography

### Space Grotesk

Use for:

- Major headlines
- Large outcomes
- Section titles
- Narrative emphasis

Space Grotesk gives the brand a contemporary product-leadership voice without losing clarity or warmth.

### DM Sans

Use for:

- Body copy
- Navigation
- Buttons
- Product and case-study descriptions
- Data explanations

DM Sans is clean, open, and highly readable. Its softer geometry keeps the modern system from feeling cold.

### IBM Plex Mono

Use sparingly for:

- Eyebrows
- Data labels
- Case-study categories
- Technical annotations

## Color system

| Role | Color | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Warm paper | Cream | `#F5F1E9` | Primary background |
| Surface | White | `#FFFDF8` | Cards and reading surfaces |
| Primary ink | Near black | `#111A2A` | Body copy |
| Systems navy | Navy | `#142A52` | Headlines, dark sections, primary actions |
| Signal | Orange-red | `#EB5735` | Calls to action, decisive moments |
| Evidence | Blue | `#3478F6` | Data, product states, measurable proof |
| Progress | Green | `#15866A` | Success and improvement |
| Rule | Warm gray | `#D9D2C5` | Borders and structure |

The orange-red communicates initiative. Blue is reserved for proof. Green is reserved for successful movement. This makes color meaningful instead of decorative.

## Layout principles

- Wide editorial canvas with a 1200-pixel maximum content width
- Strong asymmetry in hero and flagship sections
- Large type paired with compact evidence
- Fewer cards, with more variation in scale and composition
- Real artifacts before decorative visuals
- Generous negative space
- Clear borders and grids that reflect process structure
- Rounded corners used selectively, not everywhere

## Image direction

Use:

- Real facilitation photographs
- Product screenshots
- Annotated interface crops
- Course stills
- Storyboards
- Readiness charts
- Behind-the-scenes production images

Avoid:

- Stock office photography
- AI-generated people
- Generic abstract 3D objects
- Decorative mockups that obscure the actual work

## Signature visual motifs

- Dotted fields representing distributed teams
- Intersecting circles representing connected systems
- Fine grid lines representing operating structure
- Large editorial outcome numbers
- Small mono labels paired with human headlines

## Core message hierarchy

1. State the operational problem.
2. Name Danielle's role and the people involved.
3. Show the artifact or workflow.
4. Present the result and comparison basis.
5. State the limit on attribution or the evidence still missing.
