The challenge

The problem was not a lack of training. The pieces simply did not work together.

Orientation, role learning, manager coaching, and operational answers were disconnected. Completion did not always happen on time, readiness depended heavily on the local manager, and new employees often knew the material had been covered without knowing where to find it when the real question appeared.

The redesign treated onboarding as a performance system rather than a collection of courses. The goal was not simply to complete training. It was to help a new hire reach independent, observable performance faster—and to keep supporting them after orientation ended.

The connected system

Five parts worked together instead of competing for attention.

01

Revised orientation

Shorter, clearer foundations tied to the guest journey and operational expectations.

02

Role-based learning plan

Every learner received the modules, practice, and standards relevant to the work they would perform.

03

Manager certification

Observable skills and formal sign-offs replaced vague confidence-based completion.

04

Apollo in the flow of work

New hires could retrieve approved answers without waiting for a manager or memorizing the knowledge-base structure.

05

30/60/90-day reinforcement

Learning continued after orientation through structured practice, validation, and retained-knowledge checks.

Apollo inside onboarding

Apollo kept helping after onboarding was over.

Apollo became the durable performance-support layer behind the pathway. Eighty-eight percent of new hires used it during their first 30 days, and 74% of onboarding questions were answered without manager intervention.

A note on the data

What I am comfortable claiming from this evaluation.

  • Results compare the six months before launch with the six months after launch across 412 hires at 37 corporate locations.
  • The intervention changed several connected elements at once, so the outcomes are attributed to the onboarding system rather than to one course or tool alone.
  • Manager-time figures are estimates based on observed question volume and retrieval time.
  • A separate annualized business-impact model exists, but modeled dollar value is intentionally excluded from the headline claims on this page.
