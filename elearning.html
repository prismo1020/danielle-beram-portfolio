Building a 54-module global eLearning catalog

Creating the production system and doing the hands-on work that made scalable, multilingual blended learning possible.

The challenge

Cutting new-store training and making it consistent depended on one thing the company did not yet have: a real eLearning catalog. Without it, there was nothing to shift the classroom hours into, and no way to deliver the same quality of instruction to every location.

The usual paths both had problems. Outsourcing to a production agency is expensive and slow, and an outside vendor does not know the product or the floor the way an operator does. Building it internally was the better answer, except there was no production function to build it with: no studio, no pipeline, and no one who could carry a module all the way from an idea to a deployed course.

My role

I created the portfolio strategy and production capability from nothing: intake and prioritization, instructional and visual standards, a reusable workflow, multilingual quality controls, and deployment to the LMS. I also produced the modules hands-on, from design through final delivery, without external agencies.

The leadership value is not simply that one person can make 54 modules. It is that the work became a repeatable production system tied directly to operational priorities, with consistent quality and fast iteration across five languages.

Every stage normally belongs to a separate specialist. Here they live under one roof

The approach

I treated this like running a small studio, just with one person wearing every hat.

Owned the full production pipeline end to end

Instructional design, scriptwriting, shot planning, filming, lighting, voiceover, and video editing in DaVinci Resolve, then course assembly and deployment to the LMS. Each of those is normally a separate specialist. Keeping them under one roof meant faster iteration and complete control over quality and consistency.

Operationalized AI to reach five languages without re-shooting

I piloted and put into production an AI voiceover workflow using ElevenLabs voice cloning, which let me generate narration, including across multiple languages, without booking studio time or voice talent for every version. That is what made a single-person catalog viable for a network operating in five languages.

Built the catalog to power the blended training model

These modules are the foundation the 14-to-5 training redesign stands on. Moving foundational knowledge into well-produced async courses is what freed the on-site days for hands-on practice.

The outcome

  • A 54-module catalog, built from zero and produced entirely in-house. No agency, no production vendor.
  • The backbone of the blended model that cut new-store training from 14 days to 5.
  • An estimated six-figure vendor cost avoided. A market-rate comparison suggests that outsourcing a library this size would have run well into six figures before multilingual premiums. This is an estimate, not a booked budget reduction.
  • Multilingual reach across 5 languages, delivered through an AI voiceover workflow rather than repeated studio production.
  • Full control and fast iteration, with no dependency on outside vendors to update or expand the catalog.

Estimate basis: full-service eLearning vendors commonly quote from roughly $15,000 per finished hour for courses with voiceover, with multilingual delivery charged at a further premium. Actual pricing varies by scope and production complexity.
