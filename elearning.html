Building a 54-module global eLearning catalog

Building the course library, production workflow, and multilingual process that made blended learning possible at scale.

RolePortfolio strategy, production-system design, quality standards, and hands-on production
ChallengeScaling training needed a real course library, but there was no production function
What I didBuilt and continue to produce the catalog myself, from concept to LMS, with AI-enabled multilingual voiceover
Result54 modules created in-house, 5 languages, and an estimated six-figure vendor cost avoided

The challenge

Cutting new-store training and making it consistent depended on one thing the company did not yet have: a real eLearning catalog. Without it, there was nothing to shift the classroom hours into, and no way to deliver the same quality of instruction to every location.

Outsourcing would have been expensive and slow, and an agency would have needed time to learn the product and the reality of the floor. Building internally made more sense, but there was no studio, production workflow, or course developer to take a module from an idea to the LMS.

My role

I created the portfolio strategy and the production process: intake and prioritization, instructional and visual standards, a reusable workflow, multilingual quality checks, and LMS deployment. I also produced every module hands-on, from design through final delivery, without an agency.

The larger accomplishment was not simply producing 54 modules. It was building a repeatable way to respond to operational priorities, keep quality consistent, and make updates quickly across five languages.

Every stage normally belongs to a separate specialist. Here they live under one roof

The approach

I used ADDIE as the backbone for the catalog and SAM inside the build cycle. ADDIE kept each course tied to a real performance need through analysis, design, development, implementation, and evaluation. SAM kept the work moving: I built small prototypes, put them in front of stakeholders and learners early, and revised before investing in full production.

Handled the full production workflow

I handled instructional design, scriptwriting, shot planning, filming, lighting, voiceover, and video editing in DaVinci Resolve, followed by course assembly and LMS deployment. Owning the full workflow made it easier to test an idea, spot what was not working, and revise without waiting on an outside vendor.

Operationalized AI to reach five languages without re-shooting

I tested and introduced an AI voiceover workflow using ElevenLabs voice cloning. It allowed me to create narration in multiple languages without booking new studio time or voice talent for every update, making the catalog practical to maintain across five languages.

Built the catalog to power the blended training model

These modules made the 14-to-5 training redesign possible. Moving foundational knowledge into clear, self-paced courses freed the on-site days for hands-on practice, feedback, and certification.

The outcome

  • A 54-module catalog, created and produced entirely in-house. No agency, no production vendor.
  • The backbone of the blended model that cut new-store training from 14 days to 5.
  • An estimated six-figure vendor cost avoided. A market-rate comparison suggests that outsourcing a library this size would have run well into six figures before multilingual premiums. This is an estimate, not a booked budget reduction.
  • Multilingual reach across 5 languages, delivered through an AI voiceover workflow rather than repeated studio production.
  • Full control and fast iteration, with no dependency on outside vendors to update or expand the catalog.

Estimate basis: full-service eLearning vendors commonly quote from roughly $15,000 per finished hour for courses with voiceover, with multilingual delivery charged at a further premium. Actual pricing varies by scope and production complexity.
