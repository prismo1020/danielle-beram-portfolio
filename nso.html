The challenge

New Store Opening events are short, operationally intense, and full of decisions that matter after the coach leaves. Coaches develop multiple trainees, document readiness, complete recurring reviews, coordinate with local leadership, and formally close the event with the franchise partner.

Before the portal, those activities had no single digital system, and there was no fully defined process underneath them. Critical knowledge lived in spreadsheets, paper, and individual coaches' heads. Different openings could be coached, documented, and evaluated differently, leaving the organization with limited visibility into readiness and little usable data for improving the next launch.

There was no established workflow to automate. I had to define what a well-run opening should look like, agree on the important decisions and standards, and then build a place for that process to live.

My role

I identified the gap and took ownership without being assigned the work. No requirements document, process specification, product team, engineering team, or development headcount existed.

I first created the operating model: what coaches should do across the five-day arc, what trainees must demonstrate, what the franchise partner must confirm, what information leaders need each day, and what evidence should survive after the opening closes.

I then translated that model into the information architecture, workflows, required fields, relational data, access boundaries, confirmation behavior, lifecycle rules, reporting, and field guardrails. I personally built and shipped the application.

The portal gives the opening process one dependable home. Coaches can see what needs to happen, what readiness looks like, what requires evidence, and what should be carried forward after the opening ends.

The approach

Encoded the coaching model, not just the data

Every field and workflow reflects a decision about what coaches must do, what trainees must demonstrate, what requires confirmation, and what the organization needs to retain. I converted fragmented institutional knowledge into one explicit, repeatable operating standard.

Designed secure access around field ownership

The application uses Supabase authentication, Postgres, and database-level row security. Coaches work only with openings assigned to them, while administrators retain organizational visibility. Related records inherit access through their parent opening, keeping the permission model aligned with operational ownership.

Preserved the integrity of formal confirmations

Stable record identifiers prevent duplicate checklist and sign-off entries. Names, dates, and first-confirmation timestamps create a more dependable operational history without silently replacing the original confirmation moment.

Designed for shared field devices

The portal includes a shared-iPad warning, prominent sign-out controls, automatic sign-out after inactivity, required-field validation, and guardrails that prevent actions from being recorded without an active opening.

Chose maintainability over unnecessary complexity

I built the front end in browser-native HTML, CSS, and JavaScript, with Supabase as the managed backend and GitHub Pages for deployment. With no framework, build system, or dependency tree, the application remains practical for a solo product owner to maintain.

Made every opening measurable

The portal captures competency performance, readiness checks, scenario-based assessment results, daily operational recaps, process-confusion escalations, formal confirmations, and opening outcomes in a consistent structure. For the first time, those signals could be compared across openings and used to change the curriculum, coaching emphasis, and readiness standards for future launches.

Product iteration

The portal has evolved through multiple releases based on real field use and stakeholder input. Improvements have included shared-device protections, dual-party confirmations, planned-versus-actual date capture, variance notes, expanded setup, workflow guardrails, leadership training, scorecards, and broader administrative export.

These were not isolated feature requests. Each iteration responded to a gap in how an opening was prepared, coached, documented, or reviewed.

“I have now completed three openings using the portal, and I genuinely would not want to run another opening without it… By the end of the opening, I know the work has been captured properly and that nothing important has disappeared into someone’s personal notes.”

The trainers using the system have become some of its strongest advocates, providing enthusiastic feedback about the clarity and usability of the workflow.

From isolated openings to a learning system

Previously, one opening generated activity but little structured organizational learning. The portal changed that. Sign-offs, assessments, recaps, escalations, partner reviews, and opening outcomes now create a shared evidence base.

That data makes it possible to identify recurring readiness gaps, strengthen future agendas, refine competency standards, target coach guidance, and compare what changed from one opening to the next. The result is not only a better-run opening. It is an opening process that can keep getting better.
